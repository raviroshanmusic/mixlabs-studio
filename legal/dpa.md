# Data Processing Addendum — notes before drafting

**This is a checklist, not a document.** A DPA is a contract you sign with
customers, and unlike a privacy policy it should not be self-drafted. What
follows is what yours will need to say, so you can brief a lawyer efficiently
instead of paying one to interview you.

## When you'll need it

The first enterprise customer — a studio, a streamer, a production company with
a legal department — will ask for a signed DPA during procurement. Not having
one ready stalls the deal. Self-serve customers won't ask.

## Roles

For customer content, the customer is the **data controller** and MixLabs is the
**data processor**. They decide what goes in; we process it on their
instructions. For our own account and billing records, we are the controller.

Getting this the right way round matters — it determines who owes which
obligations.

## What the DPA must cover

- **Scope and duration** of processing, and the categories of data and data
  subjects
- **Instructions** — we process only on documented instructions from the customer
- **Confidentiality** obligations on anyone with access
- **Security measures**, described specifically enough to be meaningful
- **Subprocessors** — the customer's right to the list, notice before changes,
  and a right to object
- **Assistance** with data-subject requests, breach notification, and impact
  assessments
- **Breach notification** — the window (72 hours is standard) and what the
  notice contains
- **Deletion or return** of data when the contract ends
- **Audit rights** — usually satisfied by providing documentation rather than
  on-site inspection
- **Cross-border transfer** mechanism — Standard Contractual Clauses for the EU,
  and the DPDP Act's provisions for India

## The part specific to this business

Standard DPA templates assume the processor holds names and email addresses.
MixLabs holds **unreleased feature films**. Your DPA and confidentiality terms
should reflect what an actual leak would mean, and studio customers will read
these clauses closely rather than skim them. Say so when you brief the lawyer;
it changes what they draft.
